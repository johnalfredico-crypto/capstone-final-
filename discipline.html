Rules and Regulations

Disciplinary Measures & Corrective Actions

Corrective Actions

  • Verbal/Oral Warning: A reminder/reprimand for first-time minor offenses.
  • Written Apology: A formal letter of apology required on a case-to-case basis.
  • Written Reprimand: A formal letter/notice issued by the Discipline Committee and kept on the student's record.
  • Corrective Reinforcement: The student attends classes but completes reflection/rehabilitative tasks after their last class under supervision.
  • Conference with Discipline Committee: Parents/guardians meet with school authorities regarding offenses with formal sanctions.

Categories of Administrative Penalties

  • Suspension
  • Non-readmission: Denies enrollment for the immediately succeeding term; the student may finish the current term.
  • Exclusion: Immediately drops the student from the school roll upon resolution.
  • Expulsion: Disqualifies the student from enrolling in any public or private higher education institution in the Philippines (requires CHED approval).

1. Minor Offenses

Behaviors that deviate from rules or decorum with minimal consequences.

Sanctions: Not specified in the provided policy text.

2. Major Offenses — Category A

Behaviors with greater negative implications for individuals or the school.

Sanctions: Not specified in the provided policy text.

Examples of Violations: Not specified in the provided policy text.

3. Major Offenses — Category B

Behaviors leading to damage or destruction of property or reputation.

Sanctions: Not specified in the provided policy text.

Examples of Violations: Not specified in the provided policy text.

4. Major Offenses — Category C

Behaviors causing significant injury, endangering safety/welfare, or degrading personal/institutional integrity.

Sanctions: Not specified in the provided policy text.

Examples of Violations: Not specified in the provided policy text.

5. Major Offenses — Category D

Direct violations of Philippine laws.

Sanctions: Exclusion or Expulsion.

Examples of Violations:

  • Possession or sale of prohibited drugs or illegal drug paraphernalia.
  • Testing "confirmed positive" for drugs a second time after undergoing an intervention program.
  • Carrying or possession of firearms, deadly weapons, or explosives.
  • Membership or participation in illegal rites/ceremonies (e.g., hazing, fraternities/sororities employing illegal rites).
  • Crimes involving moral turpitude (rape, forgery, estafa, murder, homicide, etc.).
  • Sexual harassment.
  • Acts of subversion, sedition, or insurgency.
